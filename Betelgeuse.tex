\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We label every frame of FC Barcelona vs Athletic Club (\code{BAR-ATH}) as home possession,
away possession, dead ball, or ungraded abstention, using a deterministic pipeline with no
trained model and no labelled data. Dead-ball frames are detected inside each half by an IFAB
Law~8 kick-off and post-goal state machine combined with physical stoppage rules on the tracking
and Smart Tagging. Within each live stretch, the team in possession is decoded by a two-state
Viterbi path over control-gated proximity evidence and regime-weighted Smart Tagging, using a
touch-aligned switching cost $C(t)$ and a post-Viterbi micro-possession filter. On the public
five-minute window the method scores \code{spellF1} 0.4533 and \code{macroF1} 0.8570, above all
four reference baselines; on three external open matches it scores a mean \code{spellF1} of 0.466.
Its full-match score is unknown to us.
\end{abstract}

\section{Approach}

The whole method is one file, \code{possession.py}, in two stages:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Dead-ball state machine} combining IFAB Law~8 half-start and post-goal kick-off
        gates with kinematic stoppage, deceleration back-dating, and restart kick-snapping rules
        executed strictly inside each half.
  \item \textbf{Control-gated Viterbi decoding} inside each live stretch over home/away states
        with a touch-aligned switching cost $C(t)$ and a $2.0\,\text{s}$ micro-possession filter.
\end{enumerate}

There is no trained model. Every constant is a football or physics prior, stated in metres and
seconds at the top of the script.

\subsection{Why this and not something else}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{4.2cm} l Y@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Option} & \textbf{Decision} & \textbf{Reason} \\
\midrule
Supervised classifier (gradient boosting on distance and speed features) & Rejected &
  It needs labels. The only labels on this match are the public five minutes, which may not be
  used for training or tuning. The three open matches we found are too few to train on and serve
  better as a held-out check. \\
Deep sequence model (CNN / LSTM over all tracks) & Rejected &
  Same label problem, plus a larger need for external data. \\
Our own detection on the video & Rejected &
  The challenge is explicitly not a tracking problem, and the provided tracking was enough to
  work from. \\
Physics state machine + Viterbi decoding & \textbf{Chosen} &
  Needs no labels, is fully explainable, and regenerates the identical file in under 10 seconds. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

The baseline uses a centred majority vote. We replaced it with a Viterbi path because the ranking
metric scores whole possessions: a majority vote flickers at window edges, whereas a Viterbi
decoder with a state-dependent switching cost $C(t)$ and mid-flight persistence bias holds the
last owner while the ball is in flight or occluded and switches only when the opponent establishes
control.

  Frames outside the two halves are written as \code{X}; all state transitions and clean-ups stay within each half. \\

\bottomrule

Design choices on Smart Tagging and missing ball observations:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Expanded Smart Tagging with defensive inversion.} All four primary offensive phases
        (\code{BUILD UP}, \code{PROGRESSION}, \code{ATTACKING TRANSITION}, \code{CREATING CHANCES},
        weight $1.0$), two territory/direct phases (\code{BALL IN FINAL THIRD}, \code{LONG BALL},
        weight $0.7$), and four defensive phases (\code{DEFENDING IN ATTACKING THIRD},
        \code{DEFENDING IN MIDDLE THIRD}, \code{DEFENDING IN DEFENSIVE THIRD},
        \code{DEFENSIVE TRANSITION}, team sign inverted) contribute to $g(t) \in [-1, 1]$,
        ignoring \code{N/A} entries.
  \item \textbf{Set-piece tags as priors and gates, not hard boundaries.} Because \code{SET PIECES}
        intervals span wide windows around restarts ($5$--$35\,\text{s}$), we use them as a
        $\pm 0.8$ initial team prior on ambiguous/missing restarts within $\pm 6\,\text{s}$ and as a
        $\pm 8\,\text{s}$ guard on live-phase walking gating, while relying on ball kinematics for
        exact boundary frames.
  \item \textbf{Carrier-aware short-gap ball interpolation.} Most missing-ball frames lie in long
        occlusions ($150$ graded gaps, median $10.7\,\text{s}$), so interpolation cannot bridge
        long gaps. We bridge gaps $\le 2.0\,\text{s}$ linearly and extend bridging up to
        $3.5\,\text{s}$ when the same team is within $2.0\,\text{m}$ before and $2.5\,\text{m}$
        after the gap; longer gaps remain \code{NaN} and rely on regime-weighted Smart Tagging.
\end{itemize}

\section{Training data}

\textbf{No training.} No model is trained, and no labels from the provided match are used.

\textbf{Labelled data from other matches, for validation only.} To estimate how the pipeline
generalises, we score it on Metrica Sports' three open sample games
(\url{https://github.com/metrica-sports/sample-data}, about 430{,}000 frames). They are used to
measure, not to fit: the only change chosen on them is the $2.0\,\text{s}$ touch rule, selected on
games 1--2 and confirmed on held-out game~3 under the fixed protocol of Section~5.2.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Labels} are derived by code from Metrica's published event data
        (\code{validation/metrica.py}); nothing was labelled by hand. Control events (pass, carry,
        set piece, recovery, shot, ball lost) give their team the ball; a ball out, a foul, or a
        shot that goes out or in starts a stoppage that lasts until the next restart. A possession
        shorter than $3\,\text{s}$ that interrupts the other team's possession is returned to
        that team, following the challenge's definition of controlled possession.
  \item \textbf{Degradation to resemble the provided tracking.} In the sample games the ball is
        missing almost only during stoppages, which would make a missing ball a perfect dead-ball
        signal. We therefore fill the native gaps and cut new ones at random, with log-normal
        lengths of median $268$ frames, until $41\%$ of frames lack the ball, and add $0.3\,\text{m}$
        of smooth ball noise. Two random gap patterns are scored per game.
  \item \textbf{Smart Tagging} does not exist for these games, so every tag-based rule runs with
        empty input in the main external scores; the tag rules are checked separately with
        synthetic tags (Section~5.2).
  \item \textbf{Credit.} The idea of validating on open matches, degrading the ball to resemble
        the automatic tracking, and returning sub-$3\,\text{s}$ spells to the surrounding team
        was adapted from other participants' public forks of the challenge repository
        (FrancescoRomeo02 ``InkSight'', MRH2818, Mahmoudmoh-cse ``Goalgorithm''). No code was
        copied; the implementation is ours.
\end{itemize}

Use of the public five-minute ground truth, stated plainly:

\begin{itemize}
  \item We inspected it next to the tracking signals at the start of development to understand
        what the labels mean (for example, that dead-ball stretches coincide with walking-pace
        player speeds and long ball gaps).
  \item All constants are round-number football/physics priors stated at the top of
        \code{possession.py}. No hyperparameter search, grid sweep, or fitting was run against the
        public five-minute window.
  \item We ran \code{python evaluation/run\_evaluation.py} strictly as a correctness and
        regression check.
\end{itemize}

\section{Possession rules implemented}

All distances are in metres on the $105.3 \times 68.0$\,m pitch; all times are at $25$ frames per
second.

\subsection{Dead ball (\code{D})}

Every half block is evaluated independently so no stoppage or clean-up crosses ungraded \code{X}
boundaries:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{IFAB Law~8 half-start \& post-goal kick-off gates:} At the start of each half's
        graded window, and after any \code{GOALS} interval (from the first stoppage inside the goal
        window or its end), play is held as \code{D} until a valid kick-off: team median
        $x$-positions are on opposite sides of the halfway line $c_x$ with $\ge 8$ tracked players
        per team on their own half ($c_x \pm 3.0\,\text{m}$), and either a tracked ball inside the
        center circle ($d_{\text{center}} < 9.15\,\text{m}$) with a player within $2.5\,\text{m}$
        is kicked ($v_{\text{ball}} > 2.0\,\text{m/s}$ or $d_{\text{center}} > 1.5\,\text{m}$),
        or---if the ball is occluded at kick-off---a live phase (\code{BUILD UP}, \code{PROGRESSION},
        \code{LONG BALL}) starts while half separation holds (using $\ge 7$ own-half players for
        occluded post-goal restarts). Pre-kick delays $\le 5$ frames at a half start are absorbed
        into the kick-off possession.
  \item \textbf{Out of bounds:} The ball is more than $0.5\,\text{m}$ beyond any touchline or goal
        line.
  \item \textbf{Lost while walking (Smart-Tag gated):} When the ball is \code{NaN}, a $2\,\text{s}$
        mean player speed below $1.7\,\text{m/s}$ triggers \code{D} only when no live phase
        (\code{BUILD UP}, \code{PROGRESSION}, \code{CREATING CHANCES}, \code{ATTACKING TRANSITION},
        \code{BALL IN FINAL THIRD}) is active, or when a \code{SET PIECES} tag sits within
        $\pm 8\,\text{s}$. During active live-tagged play without nearby set pieces, a stricter
        global stop ($< 1.35\,\text{m/s}$ for $\ge 3.0\,\text{s}$) is required. Sustained global
        walking stops ($< 1.35\,\text{m/s}$ mean and median player speed for $\ge 3.0\,\text{s}$
        with slow ball $< 5.0\,\text{m/s}$ and no live phase tag) also mark \code{D} when a dead
        ball is walked/carried to the restart spot.
  \item \textbf{Abandoned ball (slow-pass guarded):} An in-pitch ball with nearest player
        $> 3.0\,\text{m}$ is marked \code{D} only if $v_{\text{ball}} < 1.0\,\text{m/s}$ for
        $\ge 1.5\,\text{s}$ (or $< 1.5\,\text{m/s}$ for $\ge 2.2\,\text{s}$), run-median closing
        rate $\frac{d}{dt}\min(d_H, d_A) \ge -0.8\,\text{m/s}$ over $0.5\,\text{s}$, and run-median
        player speed is $< 2.2\,\text{m/s}$.
  \item \textbf{Kicker waiting over stationary set-piece ball:} Even when $1$--$2$ players stand
        within $3.0\,\text{m}$, if the ball's net displacement over a rolling $3.5\,\text{s}$ window
        is $< 1.2\,\text{m}$ ($v_{\text{ball}} < 1.0\,\text{m/s}$) and median player speed is
        $< 1.9\,\text{m/s}$, the stretch is marked \code{D}.
  \item \textbf{Whistle back-dating, restart extension \& kick snapping:} For in-pitch stoppages,
        we look back up to $4.0\,\text{s}$ for the last touch ($< 2.0\,\text{m}$) followed by mean
        player speed dropping below $2.0\,\text{m/s}$. Stoppages extend through placed-ball
        stretches ($v_{\text{ball}} < 1.5\,\text{m/s}$ for $\ge 1.0\,\text{s}$) starting within
        $5.0\,\text{s}$ unless fast active play ($> 8.0\,\text{m/s}$ with player speed
        $\ge 2.0\,\text{m/s}$) already resumed, and tracked restart ends snap within $\pm 2.0\,\text{s}$
        to the kick acceleration frame ($v_{\text{ball}} > 2.0\,\text{m/s}$ with a player
        $< 2.5\,\text{m}$ and sustained post-kick motion).
  \item \textbf{Boundary-safe clean-up:} Inside each half, live stretches $< 2.0\,\text{s}$ between
        stoppages become \code{D}, and all \code{D} intervals $< 2.0\,\text{s}$ (including half
        boundaries) are removed.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Dead-ball ownership} is never predicted; the label is \code{D}.

\subsection{Home / away (\code{H} / \code{A})}

Let $d_H(t)$ and $d_A(t)$ be the distances from the ball to the nearest home and away player,
$v_{\text{ball}}(t)$ the smoothed ball speed, and $a_{\text{ball}}(t) = |\Delta \bar{v}_{\text{ball}} / \Delta t|$
the smoothed scalar ball acceleration over $0.16\,\text{s}$. The per-frame evidence for home is
\[
  e(t) \;=\; w_{\text{ctrl}}(t)\,p(t) \;+\; w_{\text{tag}}(t)\,g(t),
\]
where
\[
  p(t) =
  \begin{cases}
    \operatorname{clip}\bigl(d_A(t) - d_H(t),\,-1,\,1\bigr) & \text{if } \min(d_H, d_A) < 3.5\ \text{m},\\
    0 & \text{otherwise,}
  \end{cases}
\]
\[
  w_{\text{ctrl}}(t) =
  \begin{cases}
    0.2 & \text{if } v_{\text{ball}}(t) > 8.0 \land a_{\text{ball}}(t) < 5.0,\\
    1.0 & \text{otherwise,}
  \end{cases}
\]
and the Smart Tagging weight switches by tracking regime: $w_{\text{tag}}(t) = 0.10$ when the
ball is tracked within $3.5\,\text{m}$ of a player, and $0.45$ when the ball is missing or
$\ge 3.5\,\text{m}$ away. Whenever a named \code{SET PIECES} tag lies within $\pm 6\,\text{s}$ of
a \code{D -> live} restart, $w_{\text{tag}}(t)\,g(t)$ is replaced by $\pm 0.8$ over the first
$2.0\,\text{s}$ on frames where proximity is missing or ambiguous ($|d_A - d_H| < 0.5\,\text{m}$
or $\min(d_H, d_A) \ge 3.5\,\text{m}$).

Within each live stretch, the Viterbi path $s_t \in \{+1\ (\text{H}), -1\ (\text{A})\}$ maximises
\[
  \sum_t s_t\,e(t) \;-\; \sum_{t > 0} \mathbf{1}[s_t \neq s_{t-1}]\,C_{s_t}(t)
  \;+\; 0.01 \sum_{t > 0} \mathbf{1}[s_t = s_{t-1} \land \text{in\_flight\_or\_nan}(t)],
\]
with touch-aligned switching cost $C_S(t)$: $16.0$ if $d_S(t) < 2.2\,\text{m}$ with
$a_{\text{ball}}(t) > 6.0\,\text{m/s}^2$, $22.0$ if $d_S(t) < 2.2\,\text{m}$ without impulse,
$45.0$ when $\min(d_H, d_A) \ge 2.5\,\text{m}$ or the ball is \code{NaN}, and $30.0$ otherwise.
Finally, a spell shorter than $2.0\,\text{s}$ ($50$ frames) is treated as a touch, not a
controlled possession (the challenge defines possession as keeping the ball ``a few seconds''): an
interior spell flanked on both sides by the opposing team is absorbed into that team, and the last
spell before a stoppage goes to the team that had the ball before it.

\subsection{Abstention (\code{X})}

Only on ungraded frames (before kick-off, half-time, after the final whistle). Every graded frame
gets \code{H}, \code{A}, or \code{D}.

On the public window, Betelgeuse achieves \code{D} F1 $0.8722$ (precision $0.9007$, recall
$0.8455$), \code{H} F1 $0.8845$, \code{A} F1 $0.8144$, alive/dead F1 $0.9700$, transition F1
$0.2778$ ($\tau = 25$), and home share error $0.0336$.

Whole match ($199{,}319$ frames): \textbf{296} predicted possessions of mean \textbf{12.1\,s};
label counts \code{H} \textbf{55,829} ($28.0\%$), \code{A} \textbf{33,516} ($16.8\%$), \code{D} \textbf{69,512}
($34.9\%$), \code{X} \textbf{40,462} ($20.3\%$). On the second-half pre-kickoff graded window
($110{,}808$--$113{,}741$), the Law~8 kick-off gate holds \textbf{2,927 / 2,934} frames as \code{D}
purely algorithmically (releasing at frame $113{,}735$), and zero \code{D} spells shorter than
$2.0\,\text{s}$ exist anywhere in either half. We cannot measure the full-match score ourselves,
and $17$ possessions is too small a sample to rank on: its bootstrap interval for spellF1 is
$0.27$--$0.64$. Before the $2.0\,\text{s}$ touch rule (Section~5.2) the public window scored
$0.4947$ with $21$ possessions; with it, $0.4533$ with $13$. By our own protocol this window did
not decide; Section~5.2 explains why the rule was kept.

\subsection{Changes tested under a fixed protocol}

Every change was chosen on external games 1--2 and confirmed on game~3, and was accepted only if it
gained at least $0.01$ spellF1 on games 1--2, did not lower game~3, and did not lower macroF1 by
more than $0.01$. Constants were round values fixed before results were seen; the public window was
never used to choose. The protocol is in \code{validation/IMPROVEMENT\_PROMPT.md}.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Accepted: $2.0\,\text{s}$ touch rule.} We predicted far more possessions than
        the truth, mostly through short spells next to stoppages and between the other team's
        spells. Raising the absorption window from $1.2$ to $2.0\,\text{s}$ and applying it to the
        last spell before a stoppage improved spellF1 in all $9$ runs ($3$ games $\times$ $3$
        random gap patterns), by $+0.012$ to $+0.017$ per game, with macroF1 unchanged. A
        $3.0\,\text{s}$ window scored higher but was rejected: the external labels are built with a
        $3\,\text{s}$ rule, so part of that gain would only reflect our own labelling. On the
        public window the rule lowered the score (Section~5) and moved the possession count from
        $21$ to $13$ against $17$; with $17$ possessions that is within noise. We kept the rule
        because over the whole match it brings the possession rate from $5.8$ to $5.0$ per live
        minute, still above the $4.2$ per live minute of the public ground truth, so it reduces
        over-splitting without overshooting.
  \item \textbf{Rejected: stoppage start at the line crossing} (the ball crosses a line, then
        tracking loses it). $+0.002$ on games 1--2, $-0.004$ on game~3. Ball-out stoppages already
        start on time; the late starts are fouls and offsides (median $1.4\,\text{s}$ late).
  \item \textbf{Rejected: set-piece anchoring} (end each stoppage at the ball's still-to-moving
        change inside a \code{SET PIECES} window). No measurable effect externally, and on BAR-ATH
        its plain form reopened two post-goal kick-off waits as live play.
  \item \textbf{Smart Tagging rules checked.} With synthetic Smart Tagging generated from the
        external games' events (realistic window lengths, misses, throw-ins as \code{N/A}), our
        tag rules add $+0.036$ spellF1 on games 1--2 and $+0.056$ on game~3, and removing any one
        rule lowers the score. The size of the gain depends on how accurate the synthetic tags
        are; it shrinks to $+0.005$ and $+0.012$ when $15\%$ of tags name the wrong team.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Whistle stoppages without deceleration.} If players keep jogging above $2.0\,\text{m/s}$
        immediately after a whistle or the ball stays tracked without slowing, the $4.0\,\text{s}$
        look-back cannot pinpoint the exact whistle frame.
  \item \textbf{Long live occlusions.} When the ball is missing for more than $3.5\,\text{s}$ during
        active play, decoding relies on Smart Tagging ($0.45\,g(t)$), which carries automatic
        tagging noise and can miss rapid un-tagged turnovers.
  \item \textbf{Throw-in team ambiguity.} Every throw-in in Smart Tagging carries \code{Team = N/A},
        so the $\pm 0.8$ set-piece restart prior cannot disambiguate throw-ins when the ball is
        occluded right after the throw.
  \item \textbf{Pre-kickoff / post-goal occlusion edges.} Half~2's tracked warm-up ball entering
        the center circle triggers the kick-off gate $7$ frames ($0.28\,\text{s}$) early, and when
        a post-goal kick-off is fully occluded (Goal~2), release defers to the next live phase
        tag.
  \item \textbf{Genuine short possessions.} A real possession under $2.0\,\text{s}$ is absorbed
        into the surrounding team, and the first spell after a restart is not filtered.
  \item \textbf{Validation coverage.} The external games have no real Smart Tagging, and their
        labels come from event data rather than corrected tracking. Their possession definition is
        about $15\%$ coarser than the public ground truth's ($3.6$--$3.9$ against $4.2$
        possessions per live minute), so they slightly favour fewer possessions. The pipeline still
        predicts more possessions than the event labels contain ($246$ against $224$ on average).
\end{itemize}

With more time we would: infer ball position from collective player convergence and velocity
gradients during long occlusions; classify restart takers at throw-ins by touchline proximity;
detect foul and offside stoppages earlier, where our starts lag most; and add more open matches
with tracking and events.

\section{Reproduction}

Requirements: Python 3.8 or newer with \code{numpy} and \code{pandas} (\code{requirements.txt}).
Developed and verified on Python 3.14, numpy 2.5.3, pandas 3.0.6.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Unpack the data bundle into \code{data/} (or set \code{MTAH\_DATA\_DIR}). Only these files
        in \code{data/tactical/} are read:
        \begin{itemize}
          \item \code{BAR-ATH\_tactical\_atd.txt}
          \item \code{BAR-ATH\_tactical\_atd.xml}
          \item \code{BAR-ATH\_tactical\_smart\_tagging.xml}
        \end{itemize}
  \item Install and run:
\end{enumerate}

\begin{verbatim}
    pip install -r requirements.txt
    python possession.py
    python evaluation/validate_submission.py evaluation/results/Betelgeuse.csv
\end{verbatim}

The run takes about $8$ seconds and deterministically writes \code{evaluation/results/Betelgeuse.csv}.

To reproduce the external validation, download Sample Games 1--3 from the Metrica repository above
into \code{validation/sample-data/} and run \code{python validation/report.py}. It never reads the
competition match.
