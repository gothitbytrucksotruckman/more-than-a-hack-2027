\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We label every frame of FC Barcelona vs Athletic Club (\code{BAR-ATH}) as home possession,
away possession or dead ball, using a deterministic pipeline with no trained model and no labelled
data. Dead-ball frames are found by physical rules on the provided tracking; within each live
stretch, the team in possession is the most likely path of a two-state Viterbi decoder with a
fixed cost for changing owner. On the public five-minute window the method scores spellF1 0.4848
and macroF1 0.8391, above all four reference baselines. Its full-match score is unknown to us.
\end{abstract}

\section{Approach}

The whole method is one file, \code{possession.py}, in two stages:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Dead-ball detection} by physical rules on the tracking data.
  \item \textbf{Possession assignment} inside each live stretch by a two-state Viterbi path
        (home / away) with a fixed cost for changing owner.
\end{enumerate}

There is no trained model. Every constant is a football or physics prior, stated in metres and
seconds at the top of the script.

\subsection{Why this and not something else}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{4.2cm} l Y@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Option} & \textbf{Decision} & \textbf{Reason} \\
\midrule
Supervised classifier (gradient boosting on distance and speed features) & Rejected &
  It needs labels. The only labels on this match are the public five minutes, which may not be
  used for training or tuning. We had no labelled tracking from other matches. \\
Deep sequence model (CNN / LSTM over all tracks) & Rejected &
  Same label problem, plus a larger need for external data. \\
Our own detection on the video & Rejected &
  The challenge is explicitly not a tracking problem, and the provided tracking was enough to
  work from. \\
Rules + Viterbi smoothing & \textbf{Chosen} &
  Needs no labels, is fully explainable, and regenerates the identical file on every run. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

The baseline uses a centred majority vote. We replaced it with a Viterbi path because the ranking
metric scores whole possessions: a majority vote still flickers at window edges, while a path with
an explicit switching cost changes owner only when the evidence for the other team outweighs that
cost. Frames with no evidence cost nothing, so the previous owner keeps the ball, which is the
challenge's own rule for loose balls.

  Frames outside the two halves are written as \code{X}. \\
Tactical homography & No &
  The ATD is already in pitch coordinates, so out-of-bounds is tested directly on those. \\
Tactical and broadcast video & No & Not needed by a tracking-only method. \\
Broadcast ATD and homography & No &
  Not aligned to the tactical timeline; mapping it back was not worth the risk. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

Tried or considered, and dropped:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Smart Tagging \code{SET PIECES} as dead-ball markers.} In the public window their
        start times sat several seconds away from the real restart, so they would move boundaries
        the wrong way.
  \item \textbf{Smart Tagging zone codes} (\code{BALL IN FINAL THIRD}, \code{PLAYERS IN THE BOX},
        \dots). Their team tag does not reliably name the team in possession.
  \item \textbf{Ball interpolation as a major component.} Almost all missing-ball frames are in
        long gaps (150 gaps on graded frames, median about 11 seconds), so interpolation can only
        bridge a tiny share. We keep a 2-second linear bridge and treat longer gaps as missing.
\end{itemize}

\section{Training data}

\textbf{None.} No model is trained and no labelled dataset of any kind is used, from this match or
any other.

Use of the public five-minute ground truth, stated plainly:

\begin{itemize}
  \item We inspected it next to the tracking signals to understand what the labels mean (for
        example, that dead-ball stretches coincide with walking-pace player speeds and long ball
        gaps).
  \item The constants were then written down once as round-number priors. No parameter search,
        sweep or fitting was run against the public window.
  \item We scored the finished pipeline on it as a correctness check (frame alignment, label
        meaning).
\end{itemize}

\section{Possession rules implemented}

All distances are in metres on the $105.3 \times 68.0$\,m pitch; all times are at 25 frames per
second.

\subsection{Dead ball (\code{D})}

A frame is dead when any of these holds:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Out of bounds:} the ball is more than 0.5\,m beyond a line.
  \item \textbf{Lost while walking:} the ball is not tracked and the mean player speed (2-second
        average) is below 1.7\,m/s.
  \item \textbf{Abandoned ball:} the ball has been stationary (below 1.5\,m/s for at least
        1 second) with no player within 3\,m. The stoppage is back-dated to the last frame a
        player was within 2\,m, looking back at most 5 seconds.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Restarts.} A stoppage continues through any stationary-ball stretch that begins within
5 seconds of it (the ball being carried to the spot and placed). The restart is the end of the
last such stretch, when the ball is played.

\textbf{Clean-up.} Live play shorter than 2 seconds between two stoppages becomes dead; stoppages
shorter than 2 seconds are removed.

\textbf{Dead-ball ownership} is not predicted; the label is \code{D}.

\subsection{Home / away (\code{H} / \code{A})}

Let $d_H(t)$ and $d_A(t)$ be the distance from the ball to the nearest home and away player in
frame $t$. The per-frame evidence for the home team is
\[
  e(t) \;=\;
  \begin{cases}
    \operatorname{clip}\bigl(d_A(t) - d_H(t),\,-1,\,1\bigr) & \text{if } \min(d_H, d_A) < 3\ \text{m},\\[2pt]
    0 & \text{otherwise,}
  \end{cases}
  \qquad + \quad 0.1\,g(t),
\]
where $g(t) \in \{-1, 0, 1\}$ is the Smart Tagging possession-phase team tag. Within each live
stretch the label sequence $s_t \in \{+1 \text{ (home)}, -1 \text{ (away)}\}$ maximises
\[
  \sum_t s_t\, e(t) \;-\; 30 \sum_t \mathbf{1}\bigl[s_t \neq s_{t-1}\bigr].
\]

In football terms:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Switching.} Changing owner costs 30 units, about 1.2 seconds of clear control by
        the other team. A touch, a deflection or a won aerial duel without follow-up does not
        reach that.
  \item \textbf{Loose balls, balls in flight and missing balls} give zero evidence, so possession
        stays with the last team.
  \item \textbf{After a restart} each live stretch is solved independently, so the first owner is
        whoever the evidence favours, not the owner before the stoppage.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Abstention (\code{X})}

Only on ungraded frames (before kick-off, half-time, after the final whistle). Every graded frame
gets \code{H}, \code{A} or \code{D}.

\section{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Whistle stoppages start late.} After a foul the ball often keeps moving, so the
        stoppage is only detected once the ball is abandoned or lost. The preceding possession is
        then too long.
  \item \textbf{False stoppages in slow play.} A ball left alone for a second (a slow pass that
        nobody attacks) can trigger the abandoned-ball rule. Two such cases appear in the public
        window.
  \item \textbf{Stationary ball in live play.} A goalkeeper holding the ball right after a
        stoppage is read as part of the stoppage.
  \item \textbf{Short possessions are missed.} Genuine possessions under about a second do not
        overcome the switching cost.
  \item \textbf{Long live gaps.} When the ball is missing during active play, the owner is simply
        held, with only the Smart Tagging to correct it.
  \item \textbf{Dead-ball share.} 43\% of graded frames are labelled dead, against 20\% in the
        public window. That is plausible for a full match but unverified.
  \item \textbf{One match.} The constants are priors, but they have only been exercised on this
        game.
\end{itemize}

With more time we would: infer the ball's position from player motion during long gaps; detect
restarts by type (throw-in at the touchline, goal kick in the six-yard box, corner) to place
boundaries more exactly; and validate the constants on labelled tracking from other matches.

\section{Reproduction}

Requirements: Python 3.8 or newer with \code{numpy} and \code{pandas} (\code{requirements.txt}).
Developed on Python 3.14, numpy 2.5.3, pandas 3.0.6.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Unpack the data bundle into \code{data/} (or set \code{MTAH\_DATA\_DIR}). Only these files
        in \code{data/tactical/} are read:
        \begin{itemize}
          \item \code{BAR-ATH\_tactical\_atd.txt}
          \item \code{BAR-ATH\_tactical\_atd.xml}
          \item \code{BAR-ATH\_tactical\_smart\_tagging.xml}
        \end{itemize}
  \item Install and run:
\end{enumerate}

\begin{verbatim}
    pip install -r requirements.txt
    python possession.py
    python evaluation/validate_submission.py evaluation/results/betelgeuse.csv
\end{verbatim}

The run takes about 15 seconds and writes \code{evaluation/results/betelgeuse.csv}. There is no
randomness, so the same data produces the same file.
